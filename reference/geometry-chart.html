Bike Frame Craft · Reference

Geometry Chart Cheat Sheet

How to read any frame's table of numbers. Example values: the 56cm Surly Disc Trucker, a benchmark steel touring bike.

The method: find your size column, then sort every row into one of two jobs — fit (serves the rider's body, grows with size) or behavior (serves the ride feel, often constant across sizes). Lengths are mm; angles are degrees from horizontal.

Seat tube length · 560 mm

Fit

Bottom bracket → top of seat tube. The classic "frame size".

Effective top tube · 575 mm

Fit

Head tube → seat tube, measured level (not along a sloping tube). The key stretched-out-ness number.

Seat tube angle · 73°

Fit

Seat tube's lean from horizontal. Places hips over the pedals; steeper = further forward.

Head tube angle · 72°

Behavior

Steering axis' lean. Steering personality; slacker = calmer.

Chainstay length · 450 mm

Behavior

Bottom bracket → rear dropout. Long = stable + heel room for panniers (race ≈ 410 mm).

Wheelbase · 1051 mm

Behavior

Front axle → rear axle; the sum of all choices. Long = steady ship (>1 m = touring).

Fork offset / rake · 45 mm

How far the front hub sits ahead of the steering axis.

Trail

What head angle + offset conspire to create: steering stability itself.

Stack & reach

Modern fit coordinates: bottom bracket → top of head tube, vertical & horizontal.
