Frame Anatomy Cheat Sheet

The eight parts of the diamond frame, at a glance. Taught in Lesson 1.

PartRuns from → toWhy a touring builder cares
Head tube Front of the frame; fork steerer spins inside it Its angle sets steering feel — critical with loaded panniers
Top tube Head tube → seat tube Its length is the backbone of rider fit and reach
Down tube Head tube → bottom bracket shell Hardest-working tube; carries most of the frame's loads
Seat tube Bottom bracket shell → saddle clamp Classic "frame size"; its angle positions the rider over the pedals
Chain stays (pair) Bottom bracket shell → rear dropouts Length decides heel clearance for panniers and cargo stability
Seat stays (pair) Top of seat tube → rear dropouts Completes the rear triangle; rack mounts often live here
Bottom bracket shell Junction of seat tube, down tube, chain stays The heart of the frame — design drawings start from it
Dropouts (pair) Slotted plates at the ends of the stays Where the rear wheel bolts in; touring frames want sturdy ones

Remember: the front "triangle" (head, top, down, seat tubes) really has four sides. The rear triangle (seat tube, chain stays, seat stays) is a true triangle. The fork steers but is not part of the frame.
