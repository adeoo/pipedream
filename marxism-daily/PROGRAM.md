# Daily Marxism, Program (v2)

A daily email course on Marxism for Moussa, taught seriously from within the tradition. Personalized: Moussa is Lebanese and Paraguayan, living in São Paulo. Palestine, Lebanon and Latin America are central threads of the whole course, never side notes and never special episodes. Each lesson also goes out in Brazilian Portuguese to Carol.

This file is the single source of truth. The weekly writer reads this file, the state file, and the previous week's lessons, then writes. Nothing written under the old program (v1) may be sent or reused; if an old-style lesson file is ever found, keep only its topic and rewrite it from scratch under these rules.

## Delivery

- English version to moussaadel97@gmail.com. Brazilian Portuguese version to ana.ruberrime@gmail.com (Carol), with the Portuguese subject line.
- One lesson per day, morning delivery around 06:00 America/Sao_Paulo. Transport is defined in README.md (currently Resend scheduled sends plus the daily delivery-check routine; an Inkbox migration is being handled separately and does not change this program).
- Subject lines: intriguing and specific. No emoji, no dashes, no "Day X" prefix, ever. Style reference: "The fishermen of Sidon". Week and day info goes only in the footer.
- Footer: a single small line at the end of the lesson body, in italics: *Week W, Day N. Daily Marxism.* (PT: *Semana W, Dia N. Daily Marxism.*)

## The reader

An intermediate beginner. He knows the rough shape of Marxism and has partially read The Communist Manifesto and Wage Labour and Capital. He cannot yet confidently explain: use value vs exchange value, surplus value, base and superstructure, dialectical materialism, Lenin's additions, the USSR-China split, dependency theory. Teach the real machinery of these ideas. Never re-introduce Marxism from zero. He is not a native English speaker: clear, normal English.

## Voice

A serious teacher who respects the reader. Calm, precise, confident. No hype, no cheerleading, no drama. Explain, do not perform.

Never do any of the following:

- Dramatic scene-setting openers ("It is the 1840s. Steam-powered factories are swallowing...")
- "Dangerous question" teasers or any manufactured suspense
- Narrator commentary like "Feel the power of that reversal", "Understanding is a weapon", "the fog lifts"
- Emojis anywhere, including subject lines
- Fixed labeled sections repeated daily (no "The lesson", "From the source", "Reflection")
- Arabic renderings of quotes
- Em dashes or long dashes of any kind, anywhere, in either language
- Engineering or machine analogies

Openings alternate between two styles. (1) Direct essay: start inside the idea with a concrete claim about the reader's own life. (2) Concrete case: a real worker, a real number, a real place, then the concept. Endings alternate between a question to think about, a one-line teaser of tomorrow, and simply stopping when the idea is finished.

Two example paragraphs in the approved voice:

> You work eight hours today. Somewhere around the middle of the afternoon, you will have produced enough value to cover your own salary. The hours after that point produce value too, but not for you. Marx built an entire theory around that unpaid stretch of the day, and once you see it, ordinary payslips start to look different.

> In 1969 the port of Beirut handled more goods than any harbour between Marseille and Bombay, and the banks downtown held deposits from half the Arab world. Almost none of that money reached the tobacco farmers of the South. To understand why a country can be rich and poor at the same time, you need one of Marx's less famous ideas, and it is today's subject.

## Length, density, terms

- About 5 minutes of reading, roughly 600 to 800 words of main text. Never pad.
- Vary density: some days one idea built deeply, some days a faster survey of connected ideas.
- Whenever a Marxist term appears (proletariat, bourgeoisie, dialectics, labour power, and so on), add a short plain-words reminder in the same or next sentence. A few words, not a paragraph. Do this even for terms defined in past lessons.

## Continuity

Lessons connect. When an earlier concept is needed, recap it in 1 or 2 sentences maximum, touching the idea, then move on. Never re-explain a past concept in full. Full re-explanation is forbidden. Each email is complete by itself; no reading assignments.

## Quotes

Use a quote from an original text only when the phrasing is famous or powerful. One per lesson at most, set apart on its own lines, always with the source (work and author) and a short plain-words explanation right after it. Some lessons have no quote; that is fine.

## Visual aids

Simple text-only visuals are encouraged when they carry meaning: arrow chains (A → B → C), a small aligned text table when comparing two things, a short labeled list showing a structure. Never images, colors, icons or decorative ASCII art. The test: if deleting the visual loses nothing, delete it.

## End of every lesson, two mandatory blocks

Before the footer, always:

1. **The argument in five lines.** The whole lesson compressed into a chain of 3 to 5 short lines connected by arrows, plain text. The reader's map.
2. **Before you close this.** One writing task of a single sentence, answerable in 30 seconds from the lesson itself. On review days it can instead ask the reader to rebuild the arrow chain from memory.

Keep both blocks shorter than they want to be.

## Email design

Plain and phone-friendly: plain text on white, one font, a title, flowing paragraphs, the quote set apart simply. No colored cards, no boxes, no horizontal lines, no visual noise. Rendering is handled by render.py; write clean markdown and trust it.

## Weekly rhythm and the three pillars

The course spine is three equal pillars: core theory (Marx's actual concepts), history (revolutions and movements, what happened and why), and Lebanon, Palestine and Latin America.

Every week has the same rhythm: 4 theory days, 1 history day, 1 region day, 1 review day. The review day rebuilds the week's ideas in a new arrangement, never a full re-explanation. Region material appears across all weeks as examples and cases inside theory days too; the region day is simply the day it leads. Thinkers like Mahdi Amel, Ghassan Kanafani, Frantz Fanon and Mariátegui appear when they genuinely connect. Debates between Marxists appear in small doses; the main thread stays clear.

## Syllabus, 12 weeks

Each week lists the theory spine, the history day and the region day. The writer sets the day order and exact titles, keeping the 4 theory, 1 history, 1 region, 1 review rhythm.

1. **The materialist starting point.** Theory: materialism vs idealism; dialectics in plain terms; class as a relation, not an income bracket; ideology and the ruling ideas. History: 1848 and the moment of the Manifesto. Region: Lebanon's sectarian system read as a class system (first meeting with Mahdi Amel).
2. **Value.** Theory: the commodity; use value vs exchange value; socially necessary labour time; labour power as a commodity. History: enclosures and primitive accumulation, how wage labour was created in England. Region: informal work in São Paulo through the value lens.
3. **Surplus value and exploitation.** Theory: surplus value; the working day; wages as a veil; commodity fetishism. History: the fight over the working day, from the Factory Acts to Haymarket and May 1st. Region: Palestinian day labourers and work under occupation.
4. **Base, superstructure and the state.** Theory: base and superstructure; modes of production; the state as an instrument of class rule; reform vs revolution. History: the Paris Commune, 1871. Region: the Lebanese state and confessionalism as modern class rule (Mahdi Amel in depth).
5. **Lenin.** Theory: spontaneity vs organization; the vanguard party; What Is to Be Done; State and Revolution. History: October 1917. Region: the founding of Arab communist parties, the Lebanese Communist Party of 1924.
6. **Imperialism.** Theory: Lenin's imperialism as the highest stage of capitalism; monopoly and finance capital; export of capital; national self-determination. History: Sykes-Picot and the partition of the Arab world. Region: Zionist colonization as primitive accumulation, land and labour, the Nakba as dispossession.
7. **National liberation.** Theory: the national question in Marxism; Fanon on colonialism and national consciousness; tricontinentalism and internationalism as material practice. History: the 1936 to 1939 Palestinian revolt, through Kanafani's study of it. Region: Ghassan Kanafani, the PFLP, Palestine in the world revolutionary imagination.
8. **Dependency.** Theory: dependency theory (Frank, Marini); unequal exchange; development of underdevelopment; Galeano's open veins as narrative of the theory. History: Dr. Francia's autarky, the López era, and the War of the Triple Alliance as the destruction of autonomous development. Region: Paraguay today, Itaipú and modern dependency.
9. **Socialism from the soil.** Theory: Mariátegui's seven essays; indigenous socialism and the ayllu; "ni calco ni copia"; the agrarian question; myth and revolutionary will. History: land and indigenous communities from colonial Peru to Mariátegui's time. Region: the MST in Brazil, land occupation as living politics.
10. **Building socialism.** Theory: the transition problem; planning vs market; Che and the hombre nuevo debate; the USSR-China split as an argument about how to build socialism. History: Cuba from Moncada to the Sierra Maestra. Region: Cuban internationalism (Angola, the doctors) and socialism under blockade, told honestly from within.
11. **Brazil.** Theory: slavery and Brazilian capitalism, race and class; Freire's pedagogy as politics; liberation theology and Marxism; the limits of reformism. History: from abolition to Vargas, the making of Brazilian labour. Region: the PT experience, gains and limits.
12. **Marxism now.** Theory: neoliberalism; ecology and Marxism; the pink tide and its aftermath. History: the fall of the USSR and what Marxists learned from it. Region: Palestine solidarity as today's internationalism. Plus a full-course review and where the reader goes from here.
