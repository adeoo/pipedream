Daily Marxism · Quiz 1

Eight days of tools. Now use them on cases you did not see.

This quiz has three parts. Part 1 is a classification drill, in two sets. Part 2 is an error hunt. Part 3 is written analysis, with model answers.

Notes about the format. The answer options are short. The length of an option does not show the correct answer. Some cases are true border cases. For these, "Grey zone" is the correct choice. Two drills have two defensible answers, and their feedback says so. There are no grades and no timers. You can try again after each wrong answer. Only you see your text.

Part 1 · Classification drill

Set A: five explanations, four labels. Ask where each explanation starts. Does it start from conditions, from ideas, or from a hidden plan? "Grey zone" is for an explanation that starts from two places at the same time.

Drill 1 of 10

Every year, thousands of young people leave the Paraguayan countryside for Asunción or Buenos Aires. Explanation: soy needs much land and few hands. Small plots cannot compete with soy. A young person there finds no land and no wage.

Drill 2 of 10

After the Beirut port explosion of August 2020, tens of thousands of Lebanese professionals emigrated. Explanation: the Lebanese are a people of emigrants. They have left for the Americas and Africa since the 1800s. It is in their culture.

Drill 3 of 10

In the Paraguayan interior, many poor workers vote for the party that governed for most of the last seventy years. Explanation: the party gives public jobs, medicines and school kits through its local bosses. A voter who leaves the party loses the boss. A voter who stays keeps a channel to the state.

Drill 4 of 10 · Two defensible answers

In December 1987 the first intifada began in Gaza and the West Bank. On the strike days that the leaflets of the uprising named, most of the workers who crossed into Israel each morning stayed home. Explanation: the leaflets reached every town, the call was clear, and the workers obeyed it.

Drill 5 of 10

Paraguay collects very little tax on rural land. Explanation: the legislators are lazy and corrupt, and they do not care about the poor.

Set B: five persons, five labels. Use only the relation to production. Do not use income, a diploma, a contract form or a nationality. There are traps in both directions.

Drill 6 of 10

A man from a village near Bethlehem crosses the checkpoint at dawn with a work permit. He lays bricks on a building site inside Israel. A labour contractor holds the permit and keeps a third of his pay.

Drill 7 of 10

A woman in Ciudad del Este owns a stall in the market. She sells imported phones. She stands at the stall eleven hours a day. Her two nephews help her for a small wage.

Drill 8 of 10

A man in Beirut owns four apartments in Jounieh. He rents them out in dollars. He does no other work.

Drill 9 of 10

A plant manager at a meatpacking company in Mato Grosso earns a high salary. He hires and dismisses workers. He sets their shifts. The head office in São Paulo can dismiss him. He owns no shares.

Drill 10 of 10 · Two defensible answers

A family in San Pedro, Paraguay, works four hectares of cassava and corn. In the soy harvest, the father and the eldest son work for wages on a large farm nearby. The wages cover more than half of what the family needs.

Part 3 · Your own analysis

This part has no shortcuts. Write your answer first. Then compare it with the model answer. Only you see your text.

Exercise 1 · Two accounts of one fact

Tens of thousands of Palestinians from the West Bank work in the construction of Israeli settlements. Some of those settlements stand on land taken from their own villages. Write two accounts of this fact. First the idealist account. Then the materialist account.

One idealist model: the workers lost their national feeling. Money matters more to them than the land. Or: the older generation had principles, and the younger one has none. The account explains the fact through the values in the heads of the workers.

One materialist model: start from what the occupation did to production. The settlements and the closures took the land and the water that the villages farmed. The permit system and the checkpoints closed the economy of the West Bank itself. Some days, the only wage within reach of a village is on the site that took the land of that village. The settlement wage is two or three times the wage in the Palestinian towns. The Israeli employer needs the labour, and the Palestinian employer has no market. The same structure that takes the land creates the worker for the site. The feeling is not absent. The worker holds it while he lays the bricks. The occupation counts on one fact: a feeling does not pay rent. Day 4 adds a question: who benefits when this fact is told as a story about values? A story about values asks for no change of conditions.

Test your version with one question: does your materialist account explain why the number of these workers rises and falls with permit quotas and closures, and not with speeches? The idealist account cannot answer, because a national feeling has no calendar.

Exercise 2 · Predict, then check against history

Paraguay, 2008. Fernando Lugo, a former bishop with no party of his own, wins the presidency. His alliance joins landless campesino movements, small left parties and the Liberal Party, a party of landowners, professionals and merchants. His central promise is land reform, in a country where a small number of owners hold most of the farmland. The Colorado Party, in power for sixty-one years, loses for the first time. Use Day 5 and Day 8 strictly. Predict what happens to the alliance when the land question becomes concrete. Then check.

Write the prediction before you look. That is the discipline.

What happened: land reform did not advance. In Congress, the Liberals and the Colorados together held most seats, and they blocked the redistribution and a tax on soy. Landless families occupied estates on their own. On 15 June 2012, the police evicted an occupation at Curuguaty, on land claimed by a Colorado businessman. The eviction ended in a shooting that killed eleven campesinos and six police officers. Within one week, Congress impeached Lugo in a trial that lasted about one day. The Liberal Party, his ally of 2008, voted with the Colorados. The Liberal vice president took office the same night. The soy and cattle federations welcomed the return of order.

The 1848 pattern, if your prediction found it: the alliance held while the demand was a change of government. The removal of the Colorados cost the Liberals nothing. The alliance broke when the demand became property: land. The propertied ally chose order. Day 8 adds the other half. The campesino movements were a class in itself with pieces of organization, but with no force able to defend the government in one week. The owning class needed no new organization at all. It already had the congress, the courts and the federations. Its consciousness was built into institutions, and it acted in one day.

An honest note: your prediction can differ and still be good analysis. Name the Paraguayan condition that the template misses. Examples: the impeachment used a legal form, not an army. The two big parties share the same land base, so the ally was never outside the owning class. Lugo had no party of his own, so the working alliance had no political instrument, which is the exact missing piece of Day 5. A template that fits everything explains nothing.

Exercise 3 · Make the concepts work on each other

In many Brazilian municipalities, a school has two kinds of teachers in the same corridor. One passed a public exam and has a stable position. The other works on a temporary contract that the education secretary renews, or does not renew, each year. Both sell their labour power to the same employer. Both teach the same children. They rarely strike together. Use Day 6, vertical dependence, to explain why this class in itself (Day 8) does not become a class for itself. Name who benefits.

One model: the temporary teacher depends vertically on the secretary and, behind the secretary, on the mayor. Her renewal is a favor, not a right. She cannot join a strike without a risk to next year. The stable teacher depends on a different channel: the career plan, the union, the law. The two channels run upward, to different points, and never meet sideways. So each half sees the other as the problem. The stable teacher says: the temporary ones break the strike. The temporary teacher says: the stable ones strike with a safe job. That is the mechanism of Amel without sects. Dependence is organized vertically, so persons in one position meet as rivals. Who benefits: the municipality. It pays the temporary teacher less, it keeps the strike small, and it needs no sect to do it. Day 8 closes the loop. Recognition would come through a struggle that includes both halves, for example a strike whose first demand is a stable position for the temporary teachers. Until then, the corridor holds a class in itself.

Your answer can be different. Test it with three questions. Does it name the patron that each half depends on? Does it name who gains from the division? Does it say what a struggle must include to join the halves? If yes, your answer used one tool of the week on the result of another. At that moment, eight lessons become a method.

Quiz 1: closed

What each part trains. Part 1: see where an explanation starts, and see the relation through each costume: a permit, a tax form, a salary, a small property. Part 2: quality control on analysis itself. The worst enemies of the method wear its uniform: the eternal identity, the spark, the automatic class. Part 3: production. Only production shows that you can use the method outside a quiz. If your Paraguay prediction found the pattern before the reveal, the eight days did their work.
