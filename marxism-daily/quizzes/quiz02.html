Daily Marxism · Quiz 2

Six days on value. Now use them on cases you did not see.

This quiz has three parts. Part 1 is a classification drill, in two sets. Part 2 is an error hunt. Part 3 is written analysis, with model answers.

Notes about the format. The answer options are short. The length of an option does not show the correct answer. Some cases are true border cases. For these, "Border case" is the correct choice. Two drills have two defensible answers, and their feedback says so. There are no grades and no timers. You can try again after each wrong answer. Only you see your text.

Part 1 · Classification drill

Set A: five events, four labels. In each case, ask one question: what happened to the socially necessary labour time of the thing? If it changed, value changed. If only money moved, only the price moved. "Border case" is for a case where the concept of value does not fit cleanly.

Drill 1 of 10

In two years, most garment factories in Bangladesh install automatic cutting machines. A shirt now takes a third less labour to make. A small workshop in Dhaka still cuts by hand, with the same workers and the same hours as before. What happened to the value of the shirts made in that small workshop?

Drill 2 of 10

Attacks on ships in the Red Sea force cargo from Asia to Europe to sail around Africa. In Beirut, a kilo of Vietnamese coffee costs thirty percent more within two months. Nothing changed on the farms or in the roasting plants.

Drill 3 of 10 · Two defensible answers

A yerba mate cooperative in Itapúa, Paraguay, goes back to slow wood drying, which takes three times the labour of the industrial method. It sells the yerba as "artisanal" at three times the usual price, and buyers pay it.

Drill 4 of 10

Brazil creates a mandatory two year certification for industrial electricians. From now on, nobody can do the job without it. Ask about the value of an electrician's labour power, the capacity to work that an electrician sells.

Drill 5 of 10

A plot of bare land in the west of São Paulo sold for a certain sum in 2010. In 2025 it sells for six times that sum. Nobody did anything to the land. Ask about the value of the plot.

Set B: five persons, four labels. In each case, ask what the person actually sells: the capacity to work for a stretch of time, a finished product, or the use of something they own. Ignore what the contract, the app or the tax form calls it.

Drill 6 of 10

A woman in Osasco cleans a different flat each day of the week. Each family pays her a fixed day rate in cash. She brings nothing but herself; the products and the machines belong to the flat.

Drill 7 of 10

A receptionist at a clinic in Curitiba makes brigadeiros at home in the evening and sells them to her colleagues, forty boxes a week. She buys the ingredients, sets the price and keeps the money.

Drill 8 of 10 · Two defensible answers

A man in Asunción drives for a ride app twelve hours a day in his own car. The app sets the price of each ride, assigns the rides and keeps a share. He pays for the fuel and the repairs. The app calls him a partner.

Drill 9 of 10

A software developer in Florianópolis has a company registered in her own name. It has one client, invoices it every month for the same sum, and she works fixed hours in the client's office, under the client's manager, on the client's projects.

Drill 10 of 10

A retired man in Tripoli, Lebanon, owns a pickup truck. A builder rents it by the month and puts his own driver in it. The owner receives the rent and does nothing else.

Part 3 · Your own analysis

This part has no shortcuts. Write your answer first. Then compare it with the model answer. Only you see your text.

Exercise 1 · Two accounts of one fact

In Boa Vista, in the north of Brazil, Venezuelan migrants who work in construction and in shops are paid, on average, well below what Brazilians earn for the same work. Write two accounts of this fact. First the account that starts from the migrants themselves. Then the account that starts from the value of labour power and from day 14.

One account that starts from the migrants: they accept less because they are desperate. Or: they are used to lower wages at home. Or: they work harder for less, which is unfair to Brazilians. All three explain the wage by something in the migrant: a state of mind, a habit, a virtue. None of them can say why the same migrant, with a Brazilian work card and two years in the city, earns more.

One account from the theory: the value of labour power is the cost of a normal life plus what workers have won and can defend. A migrant without papers can defend nothing: no labour court, no union, no right to complain. So his labour power is bought below its value, the way day 14 described for informal workers. He is also a reserve army in the strict sense: thousands more cross the border each month, so any employer can replace him tomorrow. The employer does not need to think any of this. He only needs to offer less and see that it is accepted. The wage is low because the conditions of the sale are bad, and the conditions were made by the border, the papers and the queue at the shelter, not by the character of the person selling.

Test your version with one question: does your second account explain why the gap narrows when the migrant obtains papers and a network, without anything changing in his character? The first account cannot, because a state of mind has no document number.

Exercise 2 · Predict, then check against history

Brazil, March 2020. The pandemic closes shops, offices and restaurants. Within weeks, millions of people lose formal or informal work. Restaurants switch to delivery, and the number of couriers registered on the delivery apps grows fast. The apps pay per delivery. Use day 12 and day 14 strictly. Predict what happens to the pay per delivery and to the length of a courier's day over the following months. Then check.

Write the prediction before you look. That is the discipline.

What happened: the pay per delivery did not rise, though demand for deliveries exploded. Couriers reported that they had to stay online ten to twelve hours to make what a shorter day had made before. On 1 July 2020, couriers in cities across Brazil held the first national delivery strike, the Breque dos Apps, demanding higher pay per kilometre, accident insurance and protective equipment. A second strike followed at the end of the month. The apps made small concessions on equipment and kept the pay structure.

The pattern, if your prediction found it: demand for the service rose, but the supply of labour power rose faster, because the reserve army of day 14 grew by millions in a few weeks. With thousands of new couriers ready to take any ride, the app could hold the pay per delivery flat or let it fall. Pay per piece then did the rest: each courier lengthened his own day to reach the cost of a normal life, which is the informal worker of day 14 selling labour power below its value by working the hours a body cannot sustain. The strike is the other side of the theory: a class in itself, thousands of strangers on motorbikes, meeting for the first time as a class for itself.

An honest note: a prediction that adds a condition the template misses is analysis, not failure. Examples: the apps also cut pay per kilometre at the same moment, so the fall was a decision, not only a market effect; the strike was organized through the same phones that the apps use to control the work; and the pay per delivery is not a wage on paper, so the workers had no labour law to invoke. Name what your prediction got wrong and why. That is the exercise.

Exercise 3 · Make the concepts work on each other

Day 13 told how English peasants were separated from the land. Day 14 said that informal workers press down the value of labour power for the whole class. Use the concept of day 12, the value of labour power as the cost of a normal life, to explain why dispossession was necessary for capital and why the reserve army keeps that value low today. Say what connects the enclosures of 1750 to the seamstress's fourteen hours.

One model: the value of labour power is the cost of a normal life, but a person who has land, a cow and a common wood does not need to sell labour power to reach that cost. She reaches it herself. So no capitalist could buy her capacity at any price near its value; she would ask for the whole product. Enclosure removed the other way to live. After it, the only route to a normal life ran through the sale, and the buyer could pay the cost of the life and keep the rest of the day. Dispossession is what makes the value of labour power the ceiling of the wage instead of the floor of a negotiation. The reserve army is the same mechanism kept running. As long as there are people outside the gate with no other way to live, the worker inside cannot ask for more than the cost of a normal life, and often gets less. The seamstress's fourteen hours are the enclosures arriving in Bom Retiro by way of a border, a debt and the absence of any land to go back to.

Your answer can be different. Test it with three questions. Does it say why a person with her own means of living cannot be bought at the value of labour power? Does it say what the reserve army does to the wage of the worker who already has a job? Does it name the thing that both enclosure and informality remove, the alternative? If yes, three days of the week are working on each other. That is what the quiz is for.
