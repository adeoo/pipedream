📜 **Where we are in history.** January 1848. A tiny London league of exiled German workers has asked Marx and Engels for a statement of principles. Marx, 29 years old, writes through the winter. Weeks after the *Communist Manifesto* leaves the printer, revolutions erupt across Europe — Paris, Vienna, Berlin, Milan. The pamphlet opens with a line that will echo for two centuries.

**The lesson.** *"The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles."* — صراع الطبقات, *la lucha de clases*.

Here is Marx's claim in full: in every society since the dissolution of primitive communal life, a minority has lived off the labor of a majority — master off slave, lord off serf, capitalist off worker. And in every such society, that arrangement has been contested: sometimes openly, in revolt; mostly quietly, in the daily tug-of-war over hours, rents, tributes, and bread. This struggle between exploiter and exploited is not an occasional disturbance of history. It is history's **engine**. Rome's slave revolts, the peasant wars of Europe, the French Revolution — each great turning was, beneath its banners, a battle between classes, and each ended either in "a revolutionary reconstitution of society at large, or in the common ruin of the contending classes."

Notice what this does to how you read the past — and the present. Events stop being a parade of kings, presidents, and za'ims moved by ambition or ideals. You start asking of every war, every law, every "national project": *which class wins here, and which class pays?* Ask it of the French Mandate carving up the Levant. Ask it of the War of the Triple Alliance that annihilated Paraguay. Ask it of every reconstruction contract signed in Beirut. The question is almost rude — and almost always clarifying.

And notice the hope hidden in it: if exploitation has always bred resistance, then the present order, too, is provisional. What was made by struggle can be unmade by struggle.

📖 **From the source.** The *Manifesto*'s opening:

> "Freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, lord and serf, guild-master and journeyman, in a word, oppressor and oppressed, stood in constant opposition to one another, carried on an uninterrupted, now hidden, now open fight..."

🤔 **Reflection.**
1. Take one event you were taught as national history — Lebanon's independence, Paraguay's wars, anything. Retell it to yourself in one paragraph as a story of classes rather than heroes. What becomes visible?
2. Where is the "hidden fight" happening around you right now — quiet, unnamed, but real?
