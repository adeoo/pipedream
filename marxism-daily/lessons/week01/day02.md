📜 **Where we are in history.** By the mid-1800s the old order of lords and peasants is dying. In its place, two new figures face each other across the factory floor: the owner and the worker. Marx and his lifelong collaborator Friedrich Engels — himself the son of a factory owner, who documented the slums of Manchester firsthand — set out to name what everyone could feel but no one had defined.

**The lesson.** Yesterday we learned to start from production. Today, the concept that organizes everything: **class** — طبقة, *clase*.

Most people use "class" loosely — the rich, the middle, the poor, sorted by income. Marx's definition is sharper and far more useful. Your class is not how much you earn. It is your **relation to the means of production** — the land, factories, machines, buildings, and money that make production possible.

Ask one question: *do you live by owning, or by working?* The **bourgeoisie** owns the means of production and lives from the profit that ownership yields. The **proletariat** owns nothing that can produce — no land, no workshop, no capital — and so must sell the only thing it has, its capacity to work, in exchange for a wage. One class buys labor; the other must sell it. This is not a spectrum but a *relation* — like landlord and tenant, the two positions define each other and their interests structurally collide. Every hour of unpaid overtime is a gain for one side and a loss for the other, whatever their personal virtues.

Why does the sharper definition matter? Because income sorting hides the mechanism. A Beirut taxi driver who "owns his own car" and a Paraguayan campesino renting land may out-earn a unionized port worker in a good month — yet the port worker has power the others lack: capital *needs* his labor, collectively organized, every single day. Class tells you not who has money, but who has *leverage*, and where the fault lines of a society truly run — beneath the sects, parties, and flags that claim to divide it otherwise.

📖 **From the source.** Engels, in a note to the *Communist Manifesto*:

> "By bourgeoisie is meant the class of modern capitalists, owners of the means of social production and employers of wage labour. By proletariat, the class of modern wage labourers who, having no means of production of their own, are reduced to selling their labour power in order to live."

🤔 **Reflection.**
1. Map your own family, in Lebanon and Paraguay: who lives by owning, who by working, who in between (the small shopkeeper, the professional)? 
2. Lebanon's political system sorts people by sect. What changes when you re-sort the same people by their relation to property?
