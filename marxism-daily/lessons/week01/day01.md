📜 **Where we are in history.** It is the 1840s. Steam-powered factories are swallowing Europe's countryside. Peasants who once fed themselves from the land now queue at mill gates in Manchester and Lille, working fourteen-hour days for wages that barely buy bread. Children work the looms. In this new world of astonishing wealth and astonishing misery, a young German philosopher named Karl Marx begins asking a dangerous question: *why?*

**The lesson.** Before Marx, most thinkers explained the world through *ideas*. History moved, they said, because ideas changed — because humanity became more enlightened, more rational, more Christian, more free. Philosophers called this **idealism**: the belief that consciousness drives the world.

Marx turned this on its head. Look, he said, at how people actually live. Before anyone can philosophize, they must eat, drink, have shelter and clothing. So the first fact of history is not thought — it is **production**: how human beings organize themselves to wrest a living from nature. Change how a society produces, and you change everything built on top of it — its laws, its politics, its religion, its common sense. This is **materialism**: not the love of possessions, but the insight that material life shapes mental life, and not the other way around.

*"It is not the consciousness of men that determines their existence, but their social existence that determines their consciousness."*

Feel the power of that reversal. Poverty is no longer a moral failing or God's mysterious will — it is produced, by an arrangement of people and property that can be named, studied, and changed. The miserable weaver in Silesia and the fellah in the Bekaa are not unlucky; they stand in a particular *position* within a system. And what human beings have arranged, human beings can rearrange.

Everything that follows in this course — class, exploitation, imperialism, colonization, revolution — grows from this seed. Marxism is, before anything else, a way of looking: start from the material arrangement of life, and the fog lifts.

📖 **From the source.** Marx, still in his twenties, in the *Theses on Feuerbach* (1845):

> "The philosophers have only interpreted the world in various ways; the point is to change it."

In Arabic this famous line is often rendered: الفلاسفة فسّروا العالم بطرق شتى، لكن المهمّ هو تغييره. Interpretation is not enough. Understanding is a weapon, and it is meant to be used.

🤔 **Reflection.**
1. Think of one "common sense" belief widespread in Lebanon or Paraguay — about poverty, about work, about who deserves what. Whose material interests does that belief serve?
2. Where in your own life do you see material conditions shaping what people believe, rather than beliefs shaping conditions?
