📜 **Where we are in history.** The *Manifesto* closes with a shout that became the masthead of a world movement: *Proletarier aller Länder, vereinigt euch!* In 1864 Marx helped found the International Workingmen's Association — the First International — the first attempt to organize the working class *across borders*, because capital had already organized itself across them.

**The lesson.** Today we close the week with the idea that turns analysis into a compass: **internationalism** — الأممية, *el internacionalismo*.

It follows from everything you've learned. If class, not nation, is the fundamental dividing line (Day 2), and capital "nestles everywhere, settles everywhere" (Day 4), then the working class of each country faces a *single, coordinated adversary* — and a purely national struggle is a war fought on one front against an enemy fighting on all of them. "The working men have no country," says the *Manifesto* — not that workers lack homelands they love, but that the Lebanese worker has more in common with the Paraguayan worker than either has with their own ruling class. The supposed community of "national interest" between the billionaire and his gardener is, Marxists argue, ideology (Day 6) at its purest.

Internationalism is the most slandered Marxist idea — caricatured as rootlessness, as loving everywhere and therefore nowhere. The tradition you'll meet in the coming weeks answers the slander in practice. It is Cuban doctors in villages across three continents and Cuban soldiers helping break the back of apartheid's army at Cuito Cuanavale. It is Kanafani writing Palestine into the world's revolutionary literature. It is dockers in distant ports refusing, today, to load weapons bound for the siege of Gaza. Solidarity — التضامن, *la solidaridad* — is not charity or sentiment. It is the recognition that the same system runs through the checkpoint, the plantation, and the sweatshop, and that a blow struck against it anywhere loosens its grip everywhere.

**Your week in one breath:** start from material life → find the class relation → see history as class struggle → grasp capitalism's dynamism and violence → identify the class that can end it → name the fog that hides all this → and fight as a class without borders. Next week we descend into the engine room: *Capital*, and the secret of where profit actually comes from.

📖 **From the source.** The *Manifesto*'s final lines:

> "The proletarians have nothing to lose but their chains. They have a world to win. Workers of all countries, unite!"

يا عمّال العالم اتحدوا — *¡Proletarios de todos los países, uníos!*
