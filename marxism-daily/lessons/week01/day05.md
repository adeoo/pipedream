📜 **Where we are in history.** As the factories of the 1840s multiply, something unprecedented gathers inside them: thousands of strangers, herded under one roof, ruined by the same boss, timed by the same clock. The bourgeoisie needed masses of workers. In assembling them, Marx observed, it produced something it did not intend — "its own gravediggers."

**The lesson.** Why did Marx place his hope in the **proletariat** — not in the wise, the good, or the enlightened, but specifically in those who work for wages? Not out of sentimentality. Out of analysis. The working class is revolutionary because of *where it stands*, for three reasons.

**First, its chains are radical.** The peasant could dream of owning his plot; the artisan, his workshop. The proletarian owns nothing productive and can only be freed by ending private ownership of the means of production altogether — a class whose emancipation requires everyone's. Its interest is universal.

**Second, capital concentrates it.** Slaves on scattered estates or peasants in isolated villages could rarely combine. But capitalism gathers workers by the thousands — into factories, ports, refineries — and disciplines them into cooperation. It teaches them, daily, that production is *collective*. The same organization that exploits them is rehearsal for organizing against it. Every strike is the lesson learned aloud.

**Third, its hands are on the levers.** Nothing moves without labor. When the port workers stop, the port stops. This is why, as we saw on Day 2, class is about leverage: capital can survive the anger of the poor, but not the organized refusal of the workers it depends on.

One honest caveat Marx himself insisted on: a class *in itself* — sharing a position — is not yet a class *for itself*, conscious and organized. Between the two lies everything: unions, parties, newspapers, defeats, sacrifice. That gap, and who does the work of closing it, will occupy us when we reach Lenin, Kanafani, and the organizers of Latin America.

📖 **From the source.** The *Manifesto*:

> "The advance of industry... replaces the isolation of the labourers, due to competition, by their revolutionary combination, due to association. What the bourgeoisie therefore produces, above all, are its own grave-diggers."

*Sus propios sepultureros* — its own gravediggers.

🤔 **Reflection.**
1. Where in Lebanon or Paraguay do workers hold real leverage today — ports, trucking, hospitals, Itaipú? Why is that leverage so rarely used?
2. Think of a workplace you know. What already teaches those workers cooperation — and what keeps them a class "in itself" rather than "for itself"?
