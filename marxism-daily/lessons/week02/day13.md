📜 **Where we are in history.** Marx knew a fair question was waiting: if half the day is unpaid, why doesn't the worker simply *notice*? Slavery and serfdom advertised themselves — the serf worked three days on his own strip and three on the lord's land, and no one could mistake which was which. Capitalism's innovation, Marx argues, is that it hides the same division inside a single, seamless-looking payment.

**The lesson.** The disguise is the **wage form** itself.

Your payslip says: so many hours worked, so much paid. It appears that you are paid for *all* your labor — that every hour was compensated. This is, Marx says, the appearance that makes everything else possible. "The wage-form thus extinguishes every trace of the division of the working day into necessary labour and surplus labour, into paid and unpaid labour. All labour appears as paid labour."

Compare the older systems he sets beside it. Under corvée labor, the peasant's unpaid days were visibly, humiliatingly separate — you could point at them on a calendar. The exploitation was legible, and so it bred a legible resentment. Under wage labor, the unpaid portion is dissolved into an hourly rate. What is really *payment for your capacity to work* looks like *payment for your work*, and the surplus vanishes from view.

From this single confusion grows the entire common sense of the system: that the employer "gives" jobs; that profit is the reward for risk or cleverness; that boss and worker are partners in one enterprise with shared interests; that if you are poor you did not work hard enough. Yesterday's structure, today's fog — this is the ideology of Day 6, but now you can see its precise material root. Not a lie invented by anyone. An illusion generated by the wage form itself, which everyone experiences daily as plain reality.

Marx's method throughout is exactly this: distinguish how a thing *appears* from how it *works*. The sun appears to circle the earth. The wage appears to pay for the day. Science, he says, would be superfluous if the two ever coincided.

📖 **From the source.** *Capital*, Volume I:

> "All the notions of justice held by both the worker and the capitalist, all the mystifications of the capitalist mode of production, all capitalism's illusions about freedom... have as their basis the form of appearance discussed above, which makes the actual relation invisible, and indeed presents to the eye the precise opposite of that relation."

🤔 **Reflection.**
1. Have you ever felt grateful to an employer for a job? Look at that feeling again with the wage form in mind — what is it actually made of?
2. "Profit is the reward for taking risks." Whose savings, health, and time are actually at risk when a business fails?
