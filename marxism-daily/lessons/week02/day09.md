📜 **Where we are in history.** The labor theory of value was not Marx's invention. Adam Smith and David Ricardo — the great bourgeois economists — had already argued that labor is the source of value. Marx took their own principle and followed it, ruthlessly, to a conclusion neither of them would have accepted: that profit is unpaid labor.

**The lesson.** Yesterday: what commodities have in common is human labor. Today: how much.

The obvious objection comes first, and Marx answers it immediately. If value came from labor time as such, then a lazy or unskilled worker who takes ten hours to make a chair would produce a more valuable chair than a skilled one who takes two. Absurd. So value is not determined by the time *this particular* worker took, but by the **socially necessary labor time** — the time required, on average, with the normal skill and technology of that society.

This small phrase carries enormous consequences.

It explains why value *falls* as technique improves. When a power loom lets one worker do the work of twenty, the socially necessary time for a metre of cloth collapses — and so does its value, no matter how hard the handloom weaver in Damascus or Lyon still labors. His work is unchanged; the society around him has moved, and his livelihood is annihilated by an average. This is not misfortune; it is the law of value operating exactly as it must.

It also explains capitalism's permanent, restless compulsion — the "melting into air" of Day 4. Any capitalist who produces below the socially necessary time pockets a surplus profit until competitors catch up and the average shifts down. So the machinery must always be renewed, the speed always increased, the workday always intensified. Nobody chooses this; it is imposed on every owner by competition, on pain of ruin. Capital is not a group of greedy men. It is a *system of compulsion* that acts on greedy and generous men alike.

And it explains why, in a world of stunning productivity, no one has any more time. Rising productivity under capitalism does not become leisure. It becomes cheaper commodities and the same long day.

📖 **From the source.** *Capital*, Volume I:

> "Socially necessary labour-time is the labour-time required to produce any use-value under the conditions of production normal for a given society and with the average degree of skill and intensity of labour prevalent in that society."

*El tiempo de trabajo socialmente necesario* — the invisible clock against which every producer on earth is measured.
