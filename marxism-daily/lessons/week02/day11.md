📜 **Where we are in history.** This is the center of *Capital* and the idea Marx believed would outlive him. Engels, at Marx's graveside in Highgate in 1883, named it as one of his two great discoveries: "the discovery of surplus value suddenly threw light on the problem, in trying to solve which all previous investigations, of both bourgeois economists and socialist critics, had been groping in the dark."

**The lesson.** Yesterday's gap has a name: **surplus value** — فائض القيمة, *plusvalía*, *mais-valia*.

Picture the working day as a line. In the first stretch — call it **necessary labor** — the worker produces value equal to his own wage. Had he stopped there, the exchange would be exactly even; nobody would have gained anything. But he does not stop, because he sold his day, not his four hours. Every hour after that is **surplus labor**, and the value it creates belongs, by the property laws of the system, to the man who owns the workshop. That unpaid portion is surplus value. Profit, interest, rent, dividends — all of it is carved from this one source.

Now see why Marxists call the wage relation **exploitation** — استغلال, *explotación*. The word is not moral abuse in Marx's usage; it is a technical description, as neutral as "combustion." No one need be cruel. The contract can be freely signed, the wage promptly paid, the boss personally decent. Exploitation is the *structure*: a portion of your day's labor is appropriated by someone else because they own the means of production and you do not.

And this is why capital can never simply be satisfied. Since surplus value comes only from surplus labor, there are exactly two ways to increase it. **Absolute surplus value**: lengthen the working day — more hours, fewer breaks, unpaid overtime, a second shift. **Relative surplus value**: shrink the necessary portion by making the worker's subsistence cheaper through productivity, so a smaller share of the day covers the wage. Every labor struggle you have ever heard of — the eight-hour day, the weekend, the fight over speed-ups and productivity targets — is a battle over one of these two levers.

The delivery rider paid per drop, the nurse working a double, the campesino on a soy plantation in Alto Paraná, the garment worker in a Beirut basement: different worlds, one arithmetic.

📖 **From the source.** *Capital*, Volume I:

> "The capitalist... has bought the labour-power at its day-rate. The use-value of the labour-power belongs to him during one working day. He has thus acquired the right to make the worker work for him during one day... The fact that half a day's labour is necessary to keep the worker alive during 24 hours does not in any way prevent him from working a whole day."

🤔 **Reflection.**
1. In a workplace you know, which lever is being pulled — longer/denser hours, or cheaper subsistence? What does that look like concretely?
2. If exploitation can exist with a fair wage and a kind employer, what does that tell you about reform strategies aimed at kinder bosses?
