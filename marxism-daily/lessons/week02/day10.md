📜 **Where we are in history.** Marx sets up his argument as a detective story, and here is the crime scene. The capitalist buys at full price and sells at full price — and somehow ends the day with more money than he started. Where did it come from? Not from cheating, Marx insists: assume every exchange is perfectly fair, every commodity sold at its exact value. The mystery must be solved *without* theft. And it can be.

**The lesson.** Follow the money. Capital's movement is not the simple one of the market stall — sell a thing to buy a thing you need. It is M → C → M′: money buys commodities in order to become **more money**. The whole point is the little apostrophe. Otherwise, why bother?

But value can't grow in circulation. If everyone marks up prices 10%, everyone pays 10% more; the gain cancels out. So the increase must arise in *production* — which means the capitalist must find, on the market, a commodity whose **use** creates more value than it costs to buy.

There is exactly one such commodity on earth: **labor-power** — قوة العمل, *fuerza de trabajo*. Not "labor," but the *capacity* to labor, rented by the hour.

Grasp this distinction and the whole system opens. What does labor-power cost? Like any commodity, its value is the socially necessary labor time to produce it — that is, to keep a worker alive and able to return tomorrow, and to raise the next generation: food, housing, clothing, some schooling, whatever a given society counts as the minimum. Say that costs the equivalent of four hours of labor a day.

But the capitalist has not bought four hours. He has bought the *use* of that capacity for the whole working day — eight, ten, fourteen hours. And there is nothing whatsoever that limits the working day to the four hours needed to reproduce the worker. The worker is paid the full value of his labor-power, and yet labors far beyond it.

The gap between what labor-power costs and what labor produces is where profit is born. Not a swindle — a structure. The wage can be entirely fair and the exploitation total. That is Marx's most devastating claim, and tomorrow we name it.

📖 **From the source.** *Capital*, Volume I:

> "In order to extract value out of the consumption of a commodity, our friend the money-owner must be lucky enough to find within the sphere of circulation, on the market, a commodity whose use-value possesses the peculiar property of being a source of value... and this is labour-power."

🤔 **Reflection.**
1. Estimate roughly: how many hours of your working day (or a friend's) cover the cost of simply living — and how many go beyond it?
2. Marx says the worker sells not himself but his *capacity to work*, by the hour. How does that differ from slavery or serfdom — and what does the difference conceal?
