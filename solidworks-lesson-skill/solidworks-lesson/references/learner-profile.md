# Learner profile: Adel

Read this before you write one word of a lesson. Write for this person.

## Who he is
- Automation engineer in São Paulo, Brazil. About 2 years of industry experience.
- Designs machined parts (shafts, plates, blocks, bushings), sheet metal parts, fixtures and jigs, and motion systems (conveyors, linear axes, rotating shafts).
- Uses the latest SolidWorks, English menus, **Education Edition** (drawings get the "SOLIDWORKS Educational Product. For Instructional Use Only" watermark; the license is for learning, not paid work).
- Parts go to machining shops and sheet metal shops in Brazil. He already knows 3D printing: skip it.
- **He has only his computer.** No caliper, no micrometer, no shop access for now. Real-world data must come from real drawings, maker catalog pages, datasheets and free CAD models on the internet. Never ask him to measure a physical object unless he says this has changed.

## Language
- English is his third language after Portuguese and Spanish. **He is not good with technical words in English or in Portuguese.**
- Pictures help him more than words.
- Put the Brazilian shop word in Portuguese next to each technical word the first time (for example *ajuste com interferência*, *alargador*, *mancal*, *bucha*, *rasgo*, *afastamento*, *paquímetro*, *vista explodida*, *legenda*).

## Self-ratings (at the start of the series)
- 4/5 assemblies and mates. 3/5 configurations. 2/5 Hole Wizard and Toolbox. Knows part modeling well.
- 1/5: clearance fits, ISO 286 fits, dowel pins, cosmetic vs modeled threads, hole callouts, tolerances on drawings, GD&T, sheet metal and K-factor.

## Habits he knows are wrong
No tolerance on holes where parts fit. The same ±0.1 on everything. No general tolerance note. Models real helix threads. Uses the default K-factor. No edge break notes. Avoids GD&T because he does not know how the shop reads it.

## What his shop complains about
Missing dimensions, missing tolerances, tolerances too tight and too expensive, a hole that does not fit the bearing or pin, unclear thread callouts, bends that come out the wrong size.

## How he learns best
- **By doing a real project and learning each idea at the moment he needs it** (see teaching-method.md).
- About 45 minutes of reading time per sitting. If a lesson is longer, say so honestly and split it into two sittings.
- A worked-through real case beats a rule.
- He likes: many pictures, drawings he can trust, and a Comment button on every box so he can ask questions.

## Personal preferences for replies (chat)
- Start with a short **TL;DR**.
- Friendly, plain words, little technical jargon in chat.
- Ask a short clarifying question when the request is not clear, unless he says not to.
- Do not make a published artifact unless he asks. Send lesson files as files.

## Series so far
- Session 2, "Hole tolerances and fits", version 2 is done (ISO 286 fits, bearings, dowel pins, bushings, ISO 2768-m note, SolidWorks Fit tool). Reuse its facts; do not teach it again.
- The curriculum file (`solidworks-curriculum.md`, kept outside this skill) lists what comes next.
