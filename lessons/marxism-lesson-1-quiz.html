Daily Marxism · Week 1 · Transfer test

New cases. Your tools.

None of these scenarios appeared in your emails. The question each time is whether the week's concepts — materialism, dialectics, class, ideology, 1848 — work in your hands on unfamiliar ground.

Fair warning: this round is meant to be harder. Every wrong option is a plausible misreading that trips up real readers of Marx — several are half-right. Still no grades and no timers, and the "try again" button still works. But read slowly: the difference between options is often one careful idea. ☕

Case 1 · An idea whose time came

Between 1970 and today, women entered paid work massively across most of the world, and ideas about gender roles transformed in the same period. What's the properly materialist account of how these two changes relate?

Push it further Test the method against a harder case: the abolition of slavery. Can you build both an idealist account (moral awakening) and a materialist one (what was changing in production)? Which explains the timing better?

Case 2 · Four incomes, one worker

Strictly by relation to production, which of these four people is working class?

Push it further Now the genuinely hard one: where would you place a partner at a law firm who bills clients for her own hours AND takes a share of forty associates' billings? What's she selling, and what's she skimming?

Case 3 · The doctor with a CNPJ

A hospital stops hiring physicians as employees. Each doctor must now open a one-person company and invoice the hospital monthly for the same shifts, on the same schedule, at the hospital's rates. What changed, in class terms?

Push it further Whose interest is served by the relabeling — and why might some of the doctors themselves defend it sincerely? (You have a Day 4 tool for that second question.)

Case 4 · Find the real contradiction

Which of these is an internal contradiction in the dialectical sense — two opposed necessities locked inside one arrangement?

Push it further Take the platform case forward in time: what are the possible resolutions of that contradiction? (Enshittification, collapse, regulation, a new model?) A contradiction predicts motion, not a specific ending — can you sketch two different endings from the same tension?

Case 5 · Thirty pesos

Santiago, October 2019: the metro fare rises 30 pesos (about 4 US cents) and within days Chile is in the largest protests of its history. Pundits ask: "How did 30 pesos cause all this?" What's wrong with their question?

Push it further Prediction is the hard test: name one arrangement around you today where you believe pressure is accumulating toward a threshold. What observable signs make you think so — and what would falsify your guess?

Pause & think · Build your own analysis

Take today's biggest news story. Run the week's method on it.

Three sentences, three tools: (1) What are the material conditions underneath it — who produces, who owns? (2) What had been accumulating before this "event"? (3) Whose side had the vocabulary already taken before anyone argued?

The self-check: did your three sentences mention any person's character (greedy, corrupt, heroic)? If so, push one level down: what position made that behaviour pay? Did you treat the event as starting the day it hit the news? Find its film, not its photograph. And the vocabulary question has a shortcut: imagine the same facts happening to the other side — would the headline use the same words?

Case 6 · A month of housing coverage

A news channel runs a month-long series on the housing crisis: budgeting tips for renters, interviews about interest rates, a debate on whether young people should move to smaller cities. Every fact aired is accurate. What would a Day 4 analysis say?

Push it further Invert the exercise: sketch what one week of coverage would look like if the horizon were drawn from the renters' side. Which questions would become daily, and which would quietly disappear?

Case 7 · The garage story

A billionaire's rags-to-riches biography — night shifts, a garage, relentless work — tops the bestseller lists and fills motivational seminars. The story is factually true. What ideological work does it do?

Push it further Here's a genuinely uncomfortable question: does the left have its own exception-stories that do the same kind of work? What would a symmetrical critique look like?

Case 8 · The square, six months later

A broad movement — unions, students, professionals, and the business federation — topples an autocrat together. Six months on, strikes spread: wage demands, calls to renationalize privatized industries. Reasoning strictly from 1848, what should you expect?

Push it further Now stress-test the model: can you think of a case where a cross-class alliance held and delivered? What conditions made it different — or did the property question simply never get asked?

Case 9 · The final trap

Four statements, all wearing Marxist clothes. Only one survives the week's own warnings. Which?

Push it further Each wrong statement is a real tendency inside actually-existing Marxism, not a strawman. Which of the three temptations — economism, moralism, or elitism — do you notice most in political talk around you?

Pause & think · Steelman the other side

Write the strongest objection you can to ONE claim from this week.

Not a doubt this time — an attack. Pick the claim you find most convincing and argue against it as its smartest critic would. (Example targets: "class is relation, not income," "the fare rise caused nothing," "ruling ideas serve the owning class.")

Why this matters: you can recite a claim you've merely memorized, but you can only attack well what you've actually understood — the objection has to grip the claim's real structure. If your attack drew blood, you now know precisely what the theory must answer, and the coming weeks will feel like a debate instead of a sermon. If it didn't, you've located what you haven't understood yet. Either way you win.

Transfer test: done ✔

What this round was really testing: could you explain timing with conditions (case 1), see relations through labels (cases 2–3), tell contradictions from shocks, quarrels and hypocrisy (case 4), refuse the spark and find the fuel (case 5), catch a horizon in true facts (cases 6–7), run 1848 forward (case 8), and protect the theory from its own crude versions (case 9). Wherever you slipped, that's the tool to sharpen — the coming weeks will hand you heavier material to use it on.
