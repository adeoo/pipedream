Three kinds of exercise, none of them "pick the paragraph that sounds right": rapid classification under pressure, hunting the one flawed sentence in otherwise sound analysis, and writing your own — with model answers to argue with afterwards.

Format notes, since you earned them: answer options are now one or two words, or full sentences of equal weight — length tells you nothing. Exercise 1 · Two accounts of one fact

In fifteen years, "entrepreneur" became the dream job of young people in the urban peripheries — where their parents dreamed of a carteira assinada. Write BOTH accounts: the idealist one, then the materialist one.

One idealist model: a new culture arrived — self-help books, business influencers, prosperity preaching — and changed what young people value: independence over security, hustle over stability. Minds changed, so dreams changed.

One materialist model: the carteira assinada dream died where the formal jobs died. Factories left, outsourcing and apps replaced contracts, and "entrepreneur" arrived as the name for what remained — selling on Instagram, driving, delivering — a word that turns necessity into aspiration and each unemployed person into a failed business. The influencers didn't create the dream; they gave a dignified vocabulary to conditions already in place. (Notice the Day 4 bonus: whom does it serve if precarity is called freedom?)

Check your version: did your materialist account explain the timing — why this generation and not the parents'? That's the test the idealist account keeps failing.

Exercise 2 · Predict, then check against history

Sudan, 2019. Bread prices triple; months of protests led by a professionals' association, joined by workers, students and merchants, topple a thirty-year dictator. The army steps in "temporarily" and signs a power-sharing deal with civilians. Using Day 5 strictly, write your prediction for the next three years — then check.

Commit to the prediction in writing before revealing. That's the discipline.

What happened: in October 2021, with the rotation of the power-sharing council about to pass to civilians, the generals staged a coup and dissolved the civilian government. Much of the business establishment accommodated itself to "stability"; the neighbourhood resistance committees — the movement's working-class base — fought on largely alone, and in 2023 the armed factions turned on each other with the country in between.

The 1848 rhyme, if your prediction found it: the alliance held while the demand was liberty (removing the dictator costs the propertied nothing) and broke when the question became property and power itself. The armed guardians of order played Cavaignac's part; the propertied chose them.

And the honest caveat: if your prediction differed and you can say which Sudanese condition the 1848 template misses — the army's own business empire, the foreign backers — that's not failure, that's analysis. A template that fits everything explains nothing; knowing where it strains is the skill.

Exercise 3 · Make the concepts work on each other

Day 2 says arrangements crack from internal contradiction. Day 5 shows the February alliance cracking in June. Write the February alliance's contradiction in Day 2's exact form: "It needed ___, and it needed ___, and it could not have both."

One way to write it: the alliance needed the workers' numbers on the barricades to defeat the kings — and it needed to guarantee property against those same numbers to keep its bourgeois wing. Every step that armed and organized the workers to win February prepared the confrontation of June; the alliance's victory ripened its own split. That is why the breakup wasn't a betrayal that better character could have avoided — it was the contradiction resolving, which is Day 2's whole claim about how arrangements end.

If yours differs but names two genuine opposed necessities inside the alliance — not an outside enemy, not anyone's bad faith — it's doing the work. You just used one week-1 tool to explain the output of another, which is the moment a set of lessons becomes a method.
