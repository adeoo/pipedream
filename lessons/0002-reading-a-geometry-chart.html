Reading a Geometry Chart

A frame design is a small table of numbers. Today you read a real one, from a real touring bike.

Warm-up

Two questions from Lesson 1, from memory. A wrong first try is fine. The effort makes the names stick.

What a chart is

A geometry chart is a frame written as a table. One column for each size. One row for each measurement. Lengths are in millimeters. Angles are in degrees. That is all.

The chart also matters to your mission. Your final touring frame design will be a table like this, plus a drawing. When you read charts now, you practice for the table you will write later.

A real one: the Surly Disc Trucker

The Disc Trucker is one of the most famous steel touring bikes in the world. It is a close cousin of the frame you want to build. Here are six rows of Surly's official chart, in two sizes:

Measurement56cm58cm
Seat tube length560 mm580 mm
Effective top tube575 mm590 mm
Seat tube angle73°72.5°
Head tube angle72°72°
Chainstay length450 mm450 mm
Wheelbase1051 mm1060 mm

Notice two things. First, "56cm" is a real measurement: the seat tube is 560 mm long. Second, from size 56 to size 58 the tubes grow, but the chainstays and the head angle do not change. Hold that thought.

Where the numbers live

The six rows, one line each

Seat tube · 560 mm

Fit

The "size" of the frame. It sets how tall the frame stands under you.

Effective top tube · 575 mm

Fit

How stretched out you sit. It is measured level, not along the tube. Thus a sloped frame and a classic frame compare fairly (Sheldon Brown).

Seat tube angle · 73°

Fit

It puts your hips above the pedals. A steeper angle moves you forward.

Head tube angle · 72°

Behavior

The lean of your steering axis. This is the number with the most personality. A smaller angle (more "slack") gives calmer steering. It gets its own lesson soon.

Chainstay length · 450 mm

Behavior

Long stays give stability, and heel room for panniers. Race bikes use about 410 mm. 450 mm is the touring signature.

Wheelbase · 1051 mm

Behavior

The distance from axle to axle. It is the sum of all the other choices. A long ship is a steady ship. More than one meter means touring.

The skill, in one question: does this row serve the rider's body (fit), or the ride feel (behavior)? Fit numbers grow with the size. Behavior numbers are the designer's opinion. That is why the chainstays stay at 450 mm in every size.

Your assignment

Find the geometry chart of your own bike. Search for its make, its model, and the word "geometry". Write its six numbers next to the Trucker's numbers. Then bring the two largest differences to our next chat. This is your first piece of chart criticism.
